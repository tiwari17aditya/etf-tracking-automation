# Module 1.3: ETFs & Index Funds (Mechanics, NAV, and Commodity Accumulation)

## 1. What is an Exchange Traded Fund (ETF)?
An ETF is a pooled investment vehicle holding a basket of underlying securities (stocks, bonds, physical commodities) that trades continuously on secondary stock exchanges just like an individual equity share.

Unlike traditional mutual funds that price once a day at market close:
- ETFs trade intraday in real-time.
- You can buy or sell ETFs using limit orders, stop-loss orders, and intraday execution.

## 2. Core Operational Metrics

### A. Net Asset Value (NAV) vs. Market Price
- **NAV (Net Asset Value)**: The exact fair-value sum of all underlying assets divided by total outstanding units:
  $$\text{NAV} = \frac{\text{Total Market Value of Assets} - \text{Total Liabilities}}{\text{Total Outstanding Units}}$$
- **Market Price**: The price at which units are actively changing hands on the exchange order book.
- **iNAV (Indicative NAV)**: Calculated continuously (every 10–15 seconds) during market hours.
  - **Premium**: $\text{Market Price} > \text{iNAV}$ (Buyers are overpaying).
  - **Discount**: $\text{Market Price} < \text{iNAV}$ (Buyers are getting a discount).

### B. The Creation/Redemption Arbitrage Mechanism
Authorized Participants (APs) and institutional market makers ensure Market Price stays anchored to NAV:
- If market price trades at a premium to NAV, APs buy the underlying assets, create new ETF units with the fund house, and sell those units on the exchange, driving the price back down to NAV.
- If market price trades at a discount, APs buy cheap ETF units on the exchange, redeem them with the fund house for the underlying physical assets/cash, and pocket the difference.

### C. Tracking Error & Expense Ratio
- **Total Expense Ratio (TER)**: Annual percentage fee deducted automatically from the NAV to cover fund management and licensing (typically 0.05% - 0.50% for passive ETFs vs 1.5% - 2.5% for active mutual funds).
- **Tracking Error**: The annualized standard deviation of excess returns between the ETF and its underlying benchmark index. Lower is always better.

## 3. Commodity ETFs: GoldBEES & SilverBEES Case Study

### GoldBEES (Nippon India ETF Gold BeES)
- Backed by physical gold of 99.5% purity stored in secured bank vaults.
- Provides fractional ownership of gold (approx. 0.01 grams per unit), eliminating physical storage costs, jeweler making charges, and theft risk.
- **Macro Driver**: Negative correlation with real interest rates; acts as a monetary debasement and geopolitical risk hedge.

### SilverBEES (Nippon India ETF Silver BeES)
- Backed by physical silver of 99.9% purity.
- **Dual Personality**: Part monetary hedge, part industrial raw material (critical component in solar photovoltaics, electronics, and electric vehicle circuitry).
- **Higher Volatility**: Has a beta ~1.5x to 2.5x higher than gold, meaning deeper pullbacks and more explosive rallies.
