# Module 3.3: Market Microstructure, Institutional Flows & Macroeconomic Drivers

Markets do not move simply because a company is good or bad; they move because of **supply and demand mechanics, institutional order routing, and macroeconomic capital flows**.

---

## 1. Institutional Order Flow & Delivery Analysis

### Delivery Volume vs. Intraday Churn
In equity exchanges (e.g., NSE India), total traded volume is split between:
- **Intraday Volume**: Bought and squared-off within the same session (high churn, noise).
- **Delivery Volume**: Shares actually transferred to Demat accounts for long-term holding.

$$\text{Delivery Percentage (\%)} = \frac{\text{Deliverable Quantity}}{\text{Total Traded Volume}} \times 100$$

- **High Delivery % (> 60%–70%) with Rising Price**: Indicates genuine institutional accumulation (smart money is locking away supply).
- **High Volume with Low Delivery % (< 20%)**: Pure intraday speculative churn; fragile breakout prone to rapid mean-reversion.

### Block Deals vs. Bulk Deals
- **Block Deal Window**: Separate trading window for transactions $\ge ₹10 \text{ Crores}$ (or 500,000 shares) executed at a single price between institutional parties without hitting retail order books.
- **Bulk Deal**: Any transaction in the normal order book exceeding 0.5% of total outstanding equity of the company. Reported to the public after market close.

## 2. Macroeconomic Transmission Channels

Equity markets operate within a global liquidity river controlled by central bank interest rates, currency strength, and inflation.

```
       +------------------------------------+
       |  Central Bank Raises Rates (Fed)   |
       +-----------------+------------------+
                         |
           +-------------+-------------+
           |                           |
           v                           v
+-----------------------+   +-----------------------+
|  Bond Yields Rise     |   |   Currency Strengthens|
|  (US10Y > 4.5%)       |   |   (DXY Surges)        |
+----------+------------+   +-----------+-----------+
           |                            |
           v                            v
+-----------------------+   +-----------------------+
|  Higher Discount Rate |   |  Emerging Market FII  |
|  Shrinks Tech P/E     |   |  Capital Outflows     |
+-----------------------+   +-----------------------+
```

### The 4 Major Macro Signals:
1. **US 10-Year Treasury Yield**: The risk-free benchmark for global assets. When US10Y spikes rapidly, high-duration growth stocks de-rate downwards.
2. **US Dollar Index (DXY)**: Measures USD against a basket of currencies. A rising DXY drains liquidity from emerging markets (India, Brazil, Korea).
3. **Yield Curve Inversion (10Y minus 2Y)**: Historically the most reliable recession predictor. When short-term yields exceed long-term yields, credit markets are signaling economic slowdown ahead.
4. **Commodities & Crude Oil (Brent)**: Essential inputs. For net energy importers like India, spiking crude oil expands current account deficit (CAD) and exerts rupee depreciation pressure.

## 3. Market Sentiment & Extreme Reversals
- **Warren Buffett's Maxim**: *"Be fearful when others are greedy, and greedy when others are fearful."*
- **India VIX / CBOE VIX**: Measures the market's expectation of 30-day forward volatility derived from option pricing.
  - VIX > 25–30: Market panic / Capitulation (High risk, but highest historical forward 1-year returns).
  - VIX < 11–12: Market complacency / Complacent euphoria (Tight stops, reduced leverage).
